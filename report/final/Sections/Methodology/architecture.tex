\subsection{System architecture}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \input{Figures/tikz/architecture}
  \caption{System architecture. The state machine gates the controllers on the
    filter's health signal, and the vehicle-to-camera feedback path is the
    ego-motion coupling analysed in Section~\ref{sec:results-a}.}
  \label{fig:arch}
\end{figure}

The system includes a perception, guidance and control sub-pipeline for the terminal stage of docking. Perception estimates the dock pose from the ArUco markers on the dock and publishes a filtered pose, a dock velocity estimate, and a sematic health signal. Guidance converts the dock pose and the vehicle pose into a body frame error to a target. Control regulates that error with position-based visual servo-ing (PBVS) and writes a body velocity command. A supervisory state machine sequences the mission through 4 states (with demotion paths from loss of perception):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textsc{IDLE}: vehicle not active or on hold.
  \item \textsc{COARSE}: vehicle drive to a standoff point on the dock entry axis.
  \item \textsc{FINE}: vehicle fine aligns with the dock centre pose and advance into entry.
  \item \textsc{DOCKED}: vehicle is docked within tolerance and disarmed.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \input{Figures/tikz/fsm}
  \caption{Docking state machine with the transition gates annotated.}
  \label{fig:fsm}
\end{figure}

Position based servo-ing was selected over image-based servo-ing because the marker pipeline already produces a full 6-DOF pose by fusing multiple markers of known geometry and relative positions yields a calibrated dock pose, so servo-ing in pose space costs nothing extra.

The scope boundary is the optical terminal stage, meaning the trails begin with the dock inside the camera field of view at serval metres range. Long-range acquisition (acoustic homing or an active light cue) is assumed to have guided the vehicle closer to this point and is out of scope.
