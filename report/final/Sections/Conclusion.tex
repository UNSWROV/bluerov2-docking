\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% ~1 page plus future work. Concise; restate what was done and its significance; no new results.

This thesis built and validated the terminal stage of underwater docking from passive markers and classical computer vision, the approach open to low-cost platforms: multi-marker ArUco fusion, an error-state Kalman filter with two dock-motion models, and a coarse-to-fine docking state machine. The full perception pipeline runs in the loop, and the vehicle never receives ground truth.

In simulation the system docks onto a moving target. The baseline configuration docked at every sway period down to 8~s at 0.1~m amplitude, which covers moored docks at typical continental-shelf depths in the mean New South Wales sea state. The velocity feedforward fails at short periods because the vehicle's own motion leaks into the dock measurement and is integrated into a phantom dock velocity. Tuning cannot fix it, the system's own health signals cannot see it, and the baseline is immune because it never estimates velocity.

On real underwater imagery, detection needs about 27 pixels of apparent marker size, maximum detection range is about 35 times the marker side, and detection collapses once the total optical depth exceeds about 2. In the measured pool water this sets the marker for 5~m acquisition at 182~mm (154 to 207~mm).

Together the two parts bound where this class of system works, from the motion side and from the sensing side, and they supply the numbers a dock designer needs: a capture funnel of at least 0.15~m radius tolerating about 0.1~m/s closing speed, and the marker sizing rule above. Both are measured quantities. No stage of the pipeline depends on a trained model, so the measured limits are properties of the approach itself.

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:future-work}

The immediate priority is the velocity estimator. Fusing vehicle odometry into the filter, or estimating in a vehicle-relative frame, would remove the ego-motion coupling at its source; any redesign must respect the 240~ms plant lag that sets where the instability appears. The natural companion step is hardware: Part~B measured the camera side open loop, and the next experiment is to close the loop on the real BlueROV2 in a pool.

On the dock side, the capture funnel can now be designed against the delivered interface numbers, and contact needs a real mechanical model, since the simulation's collision model is only demonstration-grade. The search phase would benefit from an active light cue: markers barely reach 5~m at practical sizes, and the linear range law makes larger markers an expensive way to buy range.

On the measurement side, an underwater camera calibration would remove the $\pm$10\% range uncertainty that dominates Part~B's error budget, and the detection measurements should be repeated in genuinely turbid water with a holdout, since the synthetic-turbidity results are an upper bound until validated there. Finally, a sway-aware dock motion model, narrowband or harmonic in place of constant velocity, may recover the feedforward benefit without the ego-motion sensitivity.
